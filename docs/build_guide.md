## Moving around ServiceNow

Six moves cover almost everything this guide asks you to do in ServiceNow. Learn them once in step 1.2; come back here whenever a step feels unclear.

### 1. Find a page with the All menu

- Click **All** at the top left.
- Type a few letters in the filter box, for example **update sets**.
- Click the matching item in the results.

Tip: Type a list name such as **cmdb_ci_computer.list** and press Enter to open that list directly.

### 2. Open a page from this guide

- Click a green **Open** button in a step. It opens the exact list or form in a new tab, already filtered to Northstar records.
- The buttons need the instance address from step 0.5. Without it they show a name to copy into the All menu instead.

Tip: Leave the guide open in one browser tab and the instance in another.

### 3. Read a list

- One row is one record.
- The count is at the bottom right, for example **1 to 20 of 248**: 248 rows in total.
- Click a column heading to sort by it.

Tip: A column you need is missing: right-click a column heading, choose **Configure**, **List Layout**, move the field to the right side and click **Save**.

### 4. Filter or group a list

- Filter: click the funnel icon at the top left of the list. Choose a field, a condition and a value, and click **Run**.
- Group: right-click a column heading and choose **Group By**.
- Undo both: click **All** in the grey line above the list.

Tip: The grey line above the list always shows which filter is active.

### 5. Open and fill in a record

- Click the blue text in the first column to open a record. Its form opens.
- A red star marks a required field.
- A field with a magnifier icon points to another record: type the first letters and pick from the list that appears.
- A small **(i)** button next to such a field shows that record; click **Open Record** to go there.
- Tabs below the fields (such as **General** or **Financial**) and lists at the bottom (such as **Roles**) hold more fields and linked records.

Tip: Grey fields cannot be changed. The system fills them in.

### 6. Save, or leave without saving

- New record: **Submit** saves it and goes back to the list.
- Existing record: **Update** saves it and goes back.
- Save and stay on the form: right-click the grey header bar of the form and choose **Save**.
- Leave without saving: click the back arrow at the top left of the form.

Tip: In the Software Asset Workspace the buttons are simply called **Save**, and **Publish** for entitlements.

### Four more things you will meet

- **Scripts - Background:** A page where an admin pastes a script and clicks **Run script**. This guide uses it for 5 scripts. Each script says at the top what it does, and none of them deletes anything.
- **Elevate role:** Some pages need a temporary extra permission. Click your picture (top right), **Elevate role**, tick **security_admin** and click **Update**. It lasts until you sign out.
- **Execute Now:** Scheduled jobs normally run at night or once a week. Open the job and click **Execute Now** to start it at once. It runs in the background; check its result in the list the step names.
- **Update sets:** The globe icon in the top bar shows which update set records your changes. Each stage starts its own.


## Stage 0: Get your ServiceNow sandbox

**Time:** 15 minutes, plus waiting time

**Goal:** Get a free personal ServiceNow instance on the Zurich release. Everything else in this project is built inside it.

**Why it matters:** A PDI is a free sandbox from ServiceNow. Nothing we do in it can touch company systems.

**Before you start**

- Choose the sandbox owner. Each developer account can have one instance, and the owner must sign in to the Developer Site at least once a week.
- Have a password manager ready. The admin password goes there and nowhere else.

### Step 0.1: Sign in to the ServiceNow Developer Site

*Where:* Developer Site

**What this step does:** Gets you into the Developer Site, the free ServiceNow website where the sandbox is requested and managed.

1. Click **Open the Developer Site** below. It opens developer.servicenow.com in a new tab.
2. Click **Sign In** at the top right.
3. Type your email address and click **Next**. Type your password and click **Sign In**.
4. No account yet: click **Sign Up** instead, fill in the form, open the email you receive and click its link. Then sign in.

- Open the Developer Site: <https://developer.servicenow.com>

**You should see:** You are signed in. Your name or picture shows at the top right.

**If not:**

- The site shows an error or says it is busy: wait a few minutes and reload. The site had outages in 2026.
- You forgot your password: click **Forgot password** on the sign-in page and follow the email.

### Step 0.2: Check whether you already have an instance

*Where:* Developer Site

**What this step does:** Each account can have only one instance. This step checks whether you already have one, so you never delete it by accident.

1. Look at the Developer Site home page. An existing instance shows as a card with its name (like dev123456) and its release.
2. Not on the home page: click your picture (top right) and look for **Manage instance** or **My instance**.
3. You have a **Zurich** instance: go straight to step 0.5.
4. You have an instance on another release and nothing in it matters to you: choose **Release instance** in its menu. Releasing deletes everything in it. Then go to step 0.3.
5. You have no instance: go to step 0.3.

**You should see:** You know whether you already have an instance, and on which release.

### Step 0.3: Request a Zurich instance

*Where:* Developer Site

**What this step does:** Asks ServiceNow for a free instance on Zurich, the release this guide is written for.

1. Click **Request Instance** at the top right of the Developer Site.
2. In the list of releases, click **Zurich**. Do not choose Australia (newer) or Brazil (newest, still in early access).
3. Click **Request**.
4. Stay on the page. Most requests finish within a few minutes, and a box then shows the instance details. Go to step 0.5.
5. If the site says you are on a waitlist, stay on it and do step 0.4 while you wait. ServiceNow sends an email when the instance is ready.
6. Write the release and today's date in the box below.

**You should see:** A box with the instance address, the user name **admin** and a password, or a message that you are on the waitlist.

**If not:**

- "We were unable to assign an instance to you": sign out, go back to the Developer Site home page, sign in and try again.
- On a waitlist: stay on it. Leaving and joining again puts you at the end. Waits of several days were common in 2026.
- Zurich is not in the list: read **Zurich is not offered** under Troubleshooting below.

**Write down:** Release requested, and on which date

### Step 5.3: Understand the per core rule

*Where:* This guide

**What this step does:** Explains in plain numbers why Windows Server needs 112 core licenses and SQL Server 24. You will be asked about this in every demo.

1. **Windows Server Standard** is licensed per core. Every physical server needs at least 16 core licenses, and every processor at least 8. Licenses come in packs of 2.
2. Our 4 application servers have 16 cores each: 64. Our 3 SQL servers have only 8 cores each, but the minimum is 16 per server: 48. Together 112. We own 112: **compliant**.
3. **SQL Server Standard** is licensed per core with at least 4 per processor. 3 servers with 2 processors of 4 cores need 24. We own 16: **8 short**. 8 cores at EUR 1,972.50 (4 packs of 2 cores at EUR 3,945) make **EUR 15,780** to pay after an audit.
4. Compare this with the Microsoft view from step 5.2 and write down what the system shows.

> Without the Microsoft rules a tool counts raw cores: 88 for Windows Server. Per core licensing minimums are why the right answer is 112.

**You should see:** You can explain 112 and 24 without notes.

**Write down:** What the system shows, and any difference

## Glossary

| Term | In plain words |
| --- | --- |
| Allocation | Assigning a license to a specific person or computer. |
| Asset tag | A short label that identifies one record. Our five purchases are tagged NSM-ENT-A to NSM-ENT-E, so scripts, reports and backups can find them. |
| Background script | A page (Scripts - Background) where an admin pastes a script and runs it once. |
| Case study | The final document that tells the project story to managers and interviewers. |
| Content library | ServiceNow's catalogue of known software products, used to recognise and clean names. |
| Content Service | An online service that keeps the content library current on paid instances. A PDI does not have it. |
| Demo data | Sample records ServiceNow can add when you switch on a product. |
| Developer Site | developer.servicenow.com, the website where you get and manage your PDI and switch on paid products for it. |
| Discovery map | The rule that connects found installations to a software model. On the model it shows as the tab Matching Discovery Models. A model without a match counts nothing. |
| Discovery model | One record for each unique combination of publisher, program name and version found on computers. |
| Elevate role | Switching on an extra admin permission, security_admin, for the current session. Some pages, such as Scripts - Background, need it. |
| Entitlement | A record of what was bought: product, how many, license metric, price and dates. |
| Evidence | Screenshots that prove a result. They go in the repository's evidence folder. |
| Import set | ServiceNow's standard way to load a spreadsheet into a table, using a transform map. |
| Installation | A record saying that a program is installed on a computer. Also called a software installation or install. |
| Instance | One company's own copy of ServiceNow, reached at its own web address. |
| License metric | What you count to know how many licenses you need: per user, per device or per processor core. |
| License position | The result of the license check for one product: compliant, under-licensed or over-licensed. |
| Normalization | Cleaning messy software names into one standard name, for example turning 'Microsoft Visio Professional 2021 x64' into Microsoft Visio 2021 Professional. |
| Normalization status | How well a discovery model was cleaned: Normalized, Partially Normalized, Publisher Normalized, Match Not Found, Manually Normalized or New. Normalized and Manually Normalized count as clean. |
| Northstar Manufacturing | Our invented company with 60 employees, 45 laptops and 12 servers. |
| PDI | Personal Developer Instance. A free ServiceNow instance for learning. It is our sandbox. |
| Per core licensing | Paying for each processor core of a server. Microsoft sets minimums per processor and per server. |
| Plugin | A package of features that you switch on in an instance. SAM Professional is a set of plugins. |
| Product type | Says whether software needs a license. Only software with product type Licensable is counted in the license check. |
| Publish | The button that switches on an entitlement. It changes the State from Build to In use; the license check ignores entitlements in Build. |
| Publisher pack | Extra licensing rules for one vendor, such as Microsoft or Oracle, included in SAM Professional. |
| Reclamation | Taking back licenses that are not used so they can be reused. |
| Reclamation rule | The condition that decides when installed software counts as unused, for example not used for three months. |
| Reconciliation | The license check. It compares what is installed and allocated with what was bought. |
| Release | A ServiceNow version. There are two a year: Zurich (2025), Australia (May 2026) and Brazil (late 2026). |
| Remediation | The options to fix a license problem, such as buying more licenses or removing installations. |
| Removal candidate | An installation the system suggests removing because the reclamation rule found it unused. |
| Role | A set of permissions given to a user, such as sam_admin or sam_user. |
| SAM | Software Asset Management: knowing what software you have and what you may use, and keeping the two in balance at the lowest cost. |
| SAM Professional | ServiceNow's paid SAM product. It cleans software names, calculates license positions and knows the rules of big vendors. |
| Scheduled job | A task the system runs automatically at set times. You can also start one at once with Execute Now. |
| ServiceNow | A cloud platform many companies use to run IT and business processes, such as requests, incidents and assets, in one system. |
| snc_read_only | A platform role that stops a user from creating, changing or deleting anything. |
| Software Asset Workspace | The modern screen in ServiceNow where SAM work is done. |
| Software model | The product as we manage it for licensing, for example Microsoft Visio Professional. It collects the matching installations. |
| Software usage | Records of when a program was last used and for how long, normally collected by an agent on each computer. |
| Synthetic data | Invented data that looks realistic but describes no real person or company. |
| True-up | Paying the vendor for use above what you bought, often after an audit. |
| Unit cost | The price of one right: one user, one device or one processor core. ServiceNow multiplies it by the number of rights to show money. |
| Update set | A container that records configuration changes so they can be exported as a backup or moved to another instance. |

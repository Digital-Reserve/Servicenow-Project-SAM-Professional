# What Software Asset Management is

## The problem

Every program a company uses comes with a license: the vendor's rules for how it may be used. The rules count different things:

| What is counted | Example |
| --- | --- |
| People | One Adobe Acrobat license per named user |
| Computers | One Visio license per computer it is installed on |
| Processor cores | SQL Server licensed per core of the server it runs on |

A company with thousands of computers soon loses track. Programs are installed and forgotten, people change jobs, servers get more cores, and purchases are spread across years and departments.

## Why it costs money

**Using more than you bought.** Vendors such as Microsoft, Oracle, IBM and Adobe audit their customers. If the audit finds more use than licenses, the company pays for the difference, often at list price. This is called a true-up.

**Buying more than you use.** Licenses that nobody uses still cost money and yearly maintenance. When a department asks for new licenses, unused ones elsewhere are often bought again instead of reused.

A simple picture: a company pays for 60 parking permits while only 35 cars park, and at the same time another car park holds 24 cars with only 16 permits. Both problems exist at once, and nobody sees either without counting.

## What Software Asset Management does

Software Asset Management (SAM) answers three questions, continuously:

1. **What do we have?** Every program on every computer, with a clean name.
2. **What may we use?** Every purchase, with its license rules.
3. **Do they match, and what does it cost?** Where we are short, where we pay for nothing, and what to do about it.

## The three possible results

| Result | Meaning | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compliant | We own at least what we use | None |
| Under-licensed | We use more than we own | Buy licenses or remove installations before an audit does it for us |
| Over-licensed | We own more than we use | Reuse the spare licenses, or stop paying maintenance |

## Benefits beyond money

- **Audit readiness.** The answer to an auditor's question is a report, not a project.
- **Security.** Unknown software is a risk. Knowing what runs where is the first step to patching it.
- **Budgeting.** Renewals and true-ups are known in advance.
